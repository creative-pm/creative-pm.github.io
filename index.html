About

A project manager who started by doing the creative work.

I started in hands-on video production at Sharecare, producing, editing, and directing shoots. From there I deliberately took on more responsibility for the people, projects, resources, and workflows behind the work. The direction has always been toward managing how creative work gets done.

As a producer at Sharecare and USA TODAY, I managed shoots, crews, production schedules, and the systems my teams used to track and find their work. In my contract work since 2022, I've owned timelines, freelancers, vendors, and delivery for organizations including Chick-fil-A, DS Smith, and the American Cancer Society.

Because I've done the creative work myself, I know what editors, designers, and writers need, where the barriers are, and how management decisions affect the speed and quality of what they make.

Intake comes first

I define the minimum information a request needs before work begins. Resolving gaps up front prevents stalls and rework later.

One place for the work

Requests, priorities, owners, status, and next steps live in one system instead of in chats and email threads.

Priorities by impact

Work is ranked by urgency, business impact, dependencies, and effort, not by whoever is asking the loudest.

Process serves the work

A new process has to make people's work easier. If it adds friction, I fix the process rather than force compliance.

Chick-fil-A

Campaign intake and production workflow

  • Contract engagement
  • In-house agency
  • Nationwide campaigns
  • $1M+ budgets

The challenge

When I started, there was very little formal project organization. Requests came in from different teams, and project details were scattered across personal chats and email threads. Work was hard to track, the creative team was interrupted constantly, and typical delivery took a week or more.

What I did

I routed every incoming request through myself so I could triage, prioritize, and assign it, which let the creatives stay focused on execution. I also managed the timeline, nationwide distribution, and quality of deliverables for campaigns with budgets over $1M, including specs, proofs, and vendors, and held every asset to Chick-fil-A's quality and creative standards.

What I built

  • Introduced Asana to the team's tool stack for the first time
  • An intake workflow that routed requests from multiple teams to a single point for triage
  • A prioritization and assignment process that kept creatives out of request handling
  • One central record of priorities, owners, status, and next steps
  • Production tracking for specs, proofs, vendors, and quality control on print and campaign deliverables

Results

Media rights, permissions, and contract operations for Video Ops

  • Nonprofit
  • Compliance
  • 10,000+ assets
  • Monday.com

The challenge

Some previously published media did not have complete release and permissions documentation, which created legal and compliance exposure for the organization. Contract intake had its own backlog of contracts that were unassigned, incorrect, or never entered at all, which put contractor payments at risk.

What I did

I assessed Video Ops production workflows and cross-team handoffs, managed contractor, freelancer, and agency contracts, and approved deliverables against release, licensing, and permissions standards. I led a freelance team of seven.

I audited previously published media and helped secure retroactive releases and permissions where documentation was incomplete. I rebuilt contract intake in Monday.com with AI handling the repetitive steps, and I handled documentation supporting BBB Accreditation.

What I built

  • Media release, licensing, and permissions infrastructure for a 10,000+ asset library
  • An audit of previously published media to find missing documentation
  • A process for securing retroactive releases and permissions
  • AI-assisted contract intake in Monday.com that pulls key terms, routes contracts, flags missing fields, and drafts summaries
  • Documentation supporting the organization's BBB Accreditation

USA TODAY

High-volume video production and team file systems

  • Digital media
  • Daily publishing
  • Team of 8
  • 2019–2022

The challenge

My team at USA TODAY published short human-interest video for digital and social audiences at high volume and near-zero budget. At peak the team published one to two videos a day. Everyone on the eight-person team produced and edited, including leadership, and the internal file and organizational system had become slow and difficult to navigate.

What I did

As Video Producer III, I owned my videos from story selection through publication, working from stories identified by the USA TODAY news team. I typically had two or three videos in production at once and turned each one around in three to four days. I also took on the reorganization of the team's entire file and organizational system.

What I built

  • A rebuilt file and organizational system for the whole department
  • A production rhythm that moved each story from selection to publication in three to four days
  • A steady weekly pipeline, usually two videos published with a third ready for the next Monday

FAQ

What recruiters usually ask.

What do you specialize in?

Creative production management. I manage the people, resources, workflows, and delivery behind creative work across digital, video, and print, and I redesign the systems that work runs on, from intake and prioritization to approvals, asset tracking, and rights compliance.

Are you a project manager or a creative professional?

I'm a project manager specializing in creative production. My background as a producer and editor helps me understand the work, but my focus is managing the people, resources, workflows, and delivery behind it.

What roles are you looking for?

Senior roles where project management, creative production, and marketing operations overlap. That includes Senior Project Manager, Creative Project Manager, Program Manager, Marketing Operations Manager, and Creative Operations Manager. I'm most interested in people-management or hybrid roles with ownership of how work moves from intake through delivery.

What leadership experience do you have?

I led a freelance team of seven at the American Cancer Society, and I've managed freelancers, contractors, and agency partners across my contract work since 2022. Earlier, at Sharecare and USA TODAY, I directed in-house editors and crews on shoots.

How do you approach a broken workflow?

I map what is actually happening rather than what the process is supposed to be, following the work from intake through completion. Then I look for where work waits, repeats, changes hands unnecessarily, or depends too heavily on one person, and I keep asking what caused each problem upstream until I reach the root cause.

Where are you based, and are you open to remote work?

I'm based in Lawrenceville, Georgia, in the Atlanta area, and I'm not relocating. I prefer remote work and I'm open to hybrid or on-site roles where being in person serves the work. I'm open to regular travel.
